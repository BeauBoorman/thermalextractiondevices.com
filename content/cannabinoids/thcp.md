### Identity notes

- THCP was isolated from an Italian medical cannabis variety (FM2) and identified alongside CBDP in Citti et al. 2019 [^1].
- Commercial "THCP products" are largely synthesized; occurrence claims on product labels are process claims, not botanical measurements.

## Thermal-extraction context

{{include includes/boiling-point-vs-device-note.md}}

THCP is a trace pre-existing constituent released by vapor-pressure-driven evaporation; no compound-specific release or degradation data exist. Its higher mouse potency does not change the thermal physics. Device chamber temperature is not sample temperature; the thermodynamic boiling point of the pure compound is not a device setpoint or a sample temperature.

## Cannabis occurrence

Trace natural constituent (fm2 variety analysis [^1]); most commercial material is synthesized. Occurrence values are batch- and report-attached. Consult [Lab Results](../lab-results.md) for batch-level measurements.

## Biosynthesis and processing

THCPA is the presumed biosynthetic precursor from the heptyl (C7) polyketide branch [^1]; THCP forms by decarboxylation on heating. Dedicated biosynthetic-enzyme studies were not located.

## Reported biological activity

### Human clinical evidence
None identified for THCP (as of 2026-08-28). Potency claims circulating in marketing derive from a mouse study, not human data.

### Preclinical animal and in vitro evidence

{{include includes/preclinical-evidence-note.md}}

In Citti et al.'s isolated-mouse work, THCP showed cannabimimetic activity (hypomobility, analgesia, catalepsy) at lower doses than Δ9-THC, consistent with higher CB1 affinity in vitro [^1]. No human data exist; the 30× "potency" marketing figure cherry-picks the CB1 binding number.

### Industry claims
"THCP is 30× stronger than THC" extrapolates one in-vitro binding assay into a consumer-potency claim; no controlled human comparison exists [^1].
