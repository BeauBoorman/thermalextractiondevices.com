# Cannabichromene (CBC)

## Identity

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Preferred name | Cannabichromene (CBC) |
| IUPAC name | 2-methyl-2-(4-methylpent-3-enyl)-7-pentylchromen-5-ol |
| CAS number | 20675-51-8 |
| PubChem CID | 30219 |
| InChIKey | UVOLYTDXHDXWJU-UHFFFAOYSA-N (connectivity; the PubChem record is the (±) racemate) [^1] |
| Molecular formula | C21H30O2 |
| Molecular mass | 314.46 g/mol (314.5) |
| Compound class | Phytocannabinoid; open-chain (non-ring-closed) chromenol meroterpenoid |
| Stereochemistry | One stereocenter at C-2; natural CBC is a scalemic mixture whose enantiomeric dominance varies by chemotype, and the PubChem identity record is the racemate — chiral methods are required for enantiomer-resolved work [^1][^2] |
| Major synonyms | Cannabichromeme [sic], pentylcannabichromene, CBC; decarboxylation product of CBCA |

### Identity notes

- Natural CBC shows distinct scalemicity grades across Cannabis chemotypes; (−)-CBC and (+)-CBC are distinct enantiomers with distinct CB2 activity [^2][^3].
- CBC shares the formula C21H30O2 with Δ9-THC, Δ8-THC, CBD, CBL, and CBT; chromatographic (and where relevant chiral) separation is required [^1].

| Boiling point | **No compound-specific measured atmospheric boiling point was located.** Vaporization enthalpy has been estimated by group-additivity/retrosynthetic methods, not by direct phase-change observation | Estimate, not a measurement [^4][^5] |

| Water solubility | Practically insoluble | Soluble in ethanol, DMSO, lipids |

| Known degradation products | CBL (photochemical cycloisomerization) [^7]; thermal products remain uncharacterized | [^7] |

> **Boiling point is not a device setpoint.** No measured atmospheric boiling point or vapor-pressure curve was located for CBC. The vaporization enthalpy estimate of Chickos (2026) is a calculation, and marketing figures such as "CBC boils at 220 °C" are device-setting folklore, not thermodynamic properties [^4][^5].

## Thermal-extraction context

{{include includes/boiling-point-vs-device-note.md}}

CBC is produced in heated material by decarboxylation of CBCA and volatilizes from botanical matrix by vapor-pressure-driven evaporation; intact-CBC release and degradation under device conditions are not directly characterized [^6]. Photochemical conversion to CBL is a storage/UV concern rather than a device-temperature mechanism [^7]. Device chamber temperature is not sample temperature; the thermodynamic boiling point of the pure compound is not a device setpoint or a sample temperature.

## Cannabis occurrence

CBC is typically a minor neutral constituent in cured drug-type flower but can reach percent-level in young plants and CBC-dominant chemotypes; measured values are batch- and report-attached [^8]. No universal cultivar claim is made. Consult [Lab Results](../lab-results.md) for batch-level measurements.

## Biosynthesis and processing

CBCA is biosynthesized from CBGA by CBCA synthase (CBCAS) [^9]; CBC forms by decarboxylation of CBCA on heating, mirroring the CBDA→CBD and THCA→THC conversions [^6]. CBC further photoisomerizes to CBL [^7].

## Reported biological activity

### Human clinical evidence
No controlled human study of isolated CBC was identified in this archive's literature review (as of 2026-08-28).

### Preclinical animal and in vitro evidence

{{include includes/preclinical-evidence-note.md}}

(−)-CBC is a CB2 receptor agonist in vitro, while (+)-CBC is not — enantiomer-resolved pharmacology [^3][^10]. In mice, CBC showed antinociceptive and anti-inflammatory effects at screened doses [^11], and antidepressant-like activity in the forced-swim paradigm [^12]. CBC shows anti-inflammatory effects in LPS-stimulated BV2 microglia at high in-vitro concentrations [^13]. None of this establishes effects in people at consumer-relevant doses.

### Industry claims
Minor-cannabinoid marketing for CBC (mood, "brain growth") remains unsupported by controlled human evidence.

## Degradation products

- Photochemical: CBL (cannabicyclol), established by irradiation of CBC [^7]
- Thermal: uncharacterized for the neutral compound [^6]
