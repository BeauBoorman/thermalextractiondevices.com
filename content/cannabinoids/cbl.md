### Identity notes

- CBL is formed photochemically from CBC (Crombie 1968 [^3]); it is a marker of UV-exposed material rather than a primary biosynthetic product.
- The PubChem record lists "Cannabitpinol"/"Cannabipinol" among synonyms — the identity of interest here is the tetracyclic CBC photoisomer; confirm CID 59444380 structure before machine use [^1].

## Thermal-extraction context

{{include includes/boiling-point-vs-device-note.md}}

CBL forms from CBC under UV irradiation rather than at device temperatures [^3]; in thermal extraction it behaves as a pre-existing minor constituent that volatilizes by vapor-pressure-driven evaporation. No compound-specific release or degradation data exist; device behavior is uncharacterized. Device chamber temperature is not sample temperature; the thermodynamic boiling point of the pure compound is not a device setpoint or a sample temperature.

## Cannabis occurrence

CBL is a trace constituent of aged or UV-exposed cannabis products; measured batches report low percentages tied to storage history rather than cultivar. Values are batch- and report-attached [^6]. Consult [Lab Results](../lab-results.md) for batch-level measurements.

## Biosynthesis and processing

CBL is not directly biosynthesized: CBCA photoisomerizes to CBLA and CBC to CBL (cycloisomerization) on irradiation [^3]; CBLA decarboxylates to CBL on heating [^5].

## Reported biological activity

### Human clinical evidence
None identified for CBL (as of 2026-08-28).

### Preclinical animal and in vitro evidence

{{include includes/preclinical-evidence-note.md}}

No dedicated in-vivo pharmacology of CBL was located in the sources available to this archive; earlier reviews describe it as largely inactive in classical cannabinoid screens [^7].

### Industry claims
None verified; CBL appears in analytics and degradation studies rather than marketing.

## Degradation products

- Uncharacterized; CBL is itself a photochemical degradation product of CBC [^3]
